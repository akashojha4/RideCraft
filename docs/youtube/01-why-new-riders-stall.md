## Shot list (three URLs)

Record in this order. Leave 2 seconds of silence at each cut.

### Shot A — The stall (about 45 s of picture)

**URL:** https://www.ridecraft.in/simulators/cockpit?practice=start-and-move-off

1. Wait for rider view. If a guide overlay appears, stay on **Starting and moving off**.
2. Follow the guide until the engine is idling in first with the clutch in (steps: Neutral → Start → Clutch in → First).
3. Open a little throttle (**↑**), then **release the clutch fast** (let go of **A**, or hold **Z**). The engine should die.
4. Hold on the stalled motorcycle for two seconds. That freeze-frame is the thumbnail if you want the bike stopped.

If the stall is fiddly, do it in **Explore freely**, then: Neutral, Start (**I**), clutch in (**A**), first (**D**), a little throttle, dump the clutch.

### Shot B — Inside the clutch (about 2 min)

**URL:** https://www.ridecraft.in/simulators/clutch-explorer

1. Use the **lever slider**. Pulled in = plates apart. Released = plates clamped.
2. Pause with the lever **in**. Then ease it out and stop where the motorcycle would start to creep (the friction zone / biting point).
3. Optionally click through the guided steps if they highlight basket, hub, and plates. Do not rush.

### Shot C — Biting point, then a clean start (about 3 min)

**URL:** https://www.ridecraft.in/simulators/clutch-throttle

1. Choose the first exercise: **Find the biting point**. First gear, no throttle. Ease the clutch out until it creeps. Hold ~2 seconds.
2. Reset. Second exercise: **Pull away without stalling**. A little throttle (15–25 %), slow clutch through the friction zone, then fully out to 10 km/h.

Then cut back to:

**URL:** https://www.ridecraft.in/simulators/cockpit?practice=start-and-move-off

3. Click **Watch how it’s done**. Let it play. Use **0.5 ×** if the button is there. Do not talk over every step — only the clutch moments.

## Spoken script

Read the lines. Square brackets are actions, not speech.

---

**[Shot A. Motorcycle on screen, engine off or idling.]**

Most people think they stall because they didn’t give it enough throttle.

That’s usually wrong.

**[Dump the clutch. Engine dies. Hold two seconds.]**

That was the clutch.

You joined a turning engine to a wheel that wasn’t moving — all at once. The engine can’t do that, so it stops.

A car hides this with an automatic or a much heavier flywheel. On a geared motorcycle, your left hand *is* the clutch. If you treat that lever like an on-off switch, you stall.

I’m going to show you what the lever actually does, inside the machine — then the same start, done slowly.

This is RideCraft. It’s a simulator, not a real motorcycle, and it is not a licence. Stay with me.

**[Cut to Shot B — clutch explorer.]**

**[Lever fully in.]**

Left hand: clutch lever. Pull it in to the bar.

Inside: that pull lifts the pressure plate. The plates come apart. The engine can keep turning, and the gearbox can sit still. That’s *disengaged*. That’s how you start in gear, how you change gear, how you stop without killing the engine.

**[Ease the lever out a little, stop in the friction zone.]**

Let it out slowly. Not all the way.

There is a short band of travel — the friction zone — where the plates are only *partly* clamped. They slip. A little of the engine’s turning reaches the rear wheel. That’s the biting point: the motorcycle just starts to creep.

**[Hold.]**

Hold it there. That’s the skill. Not throttle. This.

**[Let the lever fully out.]**

The rest of the way out, the springs clamp the plates hard. Basket and hub turn as one. *Locked.* Now the clutch is out of the way, and the throttle and the gears do the rest.

Dump it — skip that middle band — and you get the stall you saw.

**[Cut to Shot C — clutch & throttle, biting-point exercise.]**

Same idea, from a standstill, first gear, no throttle.

**[Ease clutch out until it creeps. Hold.]**

Feel that? It moves. Engine still running. That’s the biting point. Two seconds here and you’ve already beaten most first-day stalls.

**[Reset. Pull-away exercise.]**

Now a little throttle — a quarter, not a handful — *then* the same slow release.

**[Move off to ~10 km/h, clutch fully out.]**

Throttle keeps the revs from being dragged down. The clutch still does the joining. Once the two sides match speed, let the lever out fully. If you snap it at the end, it lurches. Finish the release as slowly as you started it.

**[Cut to cockpit. Click Watch how it’s done.]**

Here it is from the seat, the careful version.

Neutral. Start. Clutch in. First. A little throttle. Ease to the bite. Then out.

**[Let Watch play. Talk only on clutch-in, first, bite, away.]**

Clutch in — plates apart, so first gear won’t stall you.  
A little throttle — torque in reserve.  
Biting point — creep.  
Then out — locked, and you’re riding.

That’s the whole move-off. Repeat it until it’s boring, in a car park, with an instructor — not on the road, not from this video alone.

**[End card.]**

You can do this yourself, free, no account.
